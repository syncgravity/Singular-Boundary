# PF-I16 — Compact-group heat-kernel bridge minorization and subjective HGR tensorization

**Date:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** A uniform endpoint-conditioned bridge HGR estimate is ESTABLISHED under the stated compact-state, strictly-positive transition-density hypotheses. Its application to the PF-I16 reference link semigroup is CONDITIONAL on verifying that the link process is the compact-group heat semigroup (or another semigroup satisfying the same kernel bounds). The resulting two-block multi-cut estimate is established under those hypotheses. The all-orders support-labelled cumulant tree remains OPEN.  
**Scope:** One stationary Markov path; finite observation sets; bridge conditioning on arbitrary subfamilies of the other observed times. This is not a Yang–Mills mass-gap result and promotes no P0–P5 gate.

## 1. Why this route differs from the failed generic bridge estimate

The proposed bound
\[
\rho_{\max}((X_0,X_T),X_t)
\le\sqrt{1-e^{-2\lambda\min(t,T-t)}}
\]
is false even for stationary OU; see the companion audit
`PF_I16_HYPOCOERCIVE_THREE_POINT_BRIDGE_CONTRACTION_AUDIT_2026_11_15.md`.

A different, model-specific bridge mechanism is available when the one-link process has a strictly positive transition density on a compact state space. The key is a Doeblin minorization of the **bridge transition kernel**, uniform in its endpoint states. This controls the subjective/conditioned HGR coefficients required by maximal-correlation tensorization. It does not assert that a conditioned bridge coefficient is bounded by the unconditioned semigroup norm; that stronger assertion is false even for a finite irreducible CTMC, as shown in Section 9 of the companion audit.

## 2. Kernel hypotheses

Let \((E,\mu)\) be a compact state space with \(\mu\) a probability measure. Let \((X_t)_{t\in\mathbb R}\) be a stationary Markov process with invariant law \(\mu\), and suppose its transition kernels have a jointly continuous, strictly positive density
\[
P_t(x,dy)=p_t(x,y)\,\mu(dy),\qquad t>0.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Assume also uniform convergence of the density to equilibrium: there are \(C,\lambda>0\) and \(t_0>0\) such that
\[
\sup_{x,y\in E}|p_t(x,y)-1|\le C e^{-\lambda t},
\qquad t\ge t_0.
\tag{2.2}
\]
For each \(d>0\), define
\[
m(d)=\inf_{u\ge d}\inf_{x,y}p_u(x,y),\qquad
M(d)=\sup_{u\ge d}\sup_{x,y}p_u(x,y).
\tag{2.3}
\]
Compactness, strict positivity, continuity, and (2.2) imply
\[
0<m(d)\le1\le M(d)<\infty.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Set
\[
\delta(d)=\left(\frac{m(d)}{M(d)}\right)^2,
\qquad
\psi(d)=1-\delta(d).
\tag{2.5}
\]
Then \(0<\delta(d)\le1\) and \(0\le\psi(d)<1\) for every \(d>0\).

## 3. Uniform bridge minorization

Fix two observation times \(s<t\), with \(d=t-s>0\). Let \(\mathcal H\) be any sigma-field generated by a finite subfamily of other observations of the same Markov path.

If \(\mathcal H\) contains an observation time strictly between \(s\) and \(t\), the Markov property gives
\[
X_s\perp X_t\mid\mathcal H,
\]
so the conditional HGR coefficient is zero.

Otherwise, after conditioning on \(\mathcal H\), the only relevant boundary states are the nearest conditioned observations \(X_a=x\) and \(X_b=y\), where \(a\le s<t\le b\). Other conditioned observations outside \([s,t]\) are screened by the Markov property. If a boundary is absent, the corresponding factor below is omitted and stationarity/reversibility is used at that side.

When both boundaries are present, the conditional bridge law of \((X_s,X_t)=(U,V)\) is
\[
\frac{p_{s-a}(x,u)\,p_d(u,v)\,p_{b-t}(v,y)}
{p_{b-a}(x,y)}
\,\mu(du)\mu(dv).
\tag{3.1}
\]
The conditional transition kernel from \(U=u\) to \(V\) is
\[
K_{x,y}(u,dv)
=
\frac{p_d(u,v)p_{b-t}(v,y)}
{p_{b-s}(u,y)}\,\mu(dv),
\tag{3.2}
\]
and the marginal law of \(V\) under the same bridge is
\[
\nu_{x,y}(dv)
=
\frac{p_{t-a}(x,v)p_{b-t}(v,y)}
{p_{b-a}(x,y)}\,\mu(dv).
\tag{3.3}
\]
Their density ratio is
\[
\frac{dK_{x,y}(u,\cdot)}{d\nu_{x,y}}(v)
=
\frac{p_d(u,v)p_{b-a}(x,y)}
{p_{b-s}(u,y)p_{t-a}(x,v)}.
\tag{3.4}
\]
All three time arguments \(b-a\), \(b-s\), and \(t-a\) in (3.4) are at least \(d\). Therefore, by (2.3),
\[
\frac{dK_{x,y}(u,\cdot)}{d\nu_{x,y}}(v)
\ge\frac{m(d)^2}{M(d)^2}=\delta(d).
\tag{3.5}
\]
The same lower bound holds when one or both boundary states are absent: the corresponding ratio has one or zero factors, and \(m(d)/M(d)\ge(m(d)/M(d))^2\).

Thus, uniformly in all endpoint states and all allowed conditioning subfamilies,
\[
\boxed{
K(u,\cdot)\ge\delta(d)\,\nu_t(\cdot).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Write
\[
K(u,\cdot)=\delta(d)\nu_t(\cdot)
+(1-\delta(d))R(u,\cdot).
\tag{3.7}
\]
Because \(\nu_s K=\nu_t\), the residual kernel also transports the bridge marginal \(\nu_s\) to \(\nu_t\). For every centered \(g\in L^2(\nu_t)\),
\[
Kg=(1-\delta(d))Rg.
\]
Jensen's inequality gives \(\|Rg\|_{L^2(\nu_s)}\le\|g\|_{L^2(\nu_t)}\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\rho_{\max}(X_s,X_t\mid\mathcal H)
\le\psi(|t-s|)=1-\delta(|t-s|),
}
\tag{3.8}
\]
uniformly over the values of the conditioned observations. This is a subjective HGR bound, not merely an average over bridge endpoints.

## 4. Exponential temporal envelope

From (2.2), for all sufficiently large \(d\),
\[
m(d)\ge1-Ce^{-\lambda d},\qquad
M(d)\le1+Ce^{-\lambda d}.
\]
If \(Ce^{-\lambda d}\le1/2\), then
\[
\psi(d)
=1-\left(\frac{m(d)}{M(d)}\right)^2
\le1-\left(\frac{1-Ce^{-\lambda d}}
{1+Ce^{-\lambda d}}\right)^2
\le4C e^{-\lambda d}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
For smaller \(d\), use \(\psi(d)\le1\) and enlarge the constant. Thus there is an explicit \(C_0<\infty\), depending only on the kernel bounds, such that
\[
\boxed{\psi(d)\le C_0e^{-\lambda d},\qquad d>0.}
\tag{4.2}
\]
The function \(\psi\) is integrable on \((0,\infty)\). The rate \(\lambda\) in (4.2) is the uniform heat-kernel convergence rate; it need not equal the optimal \(L^2\) spectral gap.

## 5. Apply subjective HGR tensorization to arbitrary interlaced time sets

Let \(S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_m\}\) and \(T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_n\}\) be disjoint finite observation-time sets. Let \(\mathcal M\) be the sigma-metalgebra generated by all observations in \(S\cup T\). By (3.8), for every pair \((i,j)\),
\[
\{\sigma(X_{s_i}):\sigma(X_{t_j})\}_{\mathcal M}
\le\psi(|s_i-t_j|).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Peyre's N-against-M maximal-correlation tensorization theorem therefore applies with the nonnegative matrix
\[
E_{ij}=\psi(|s_i-t_j|),
\]
and yields
\[
\boxed{
\rho_{\max}\bigl(\sigma(X_{s_i}:i\le m),
\sigma(X_{t_j}:j\le n)\bigr)
\le\|E\|_{\ell^2_n\to\ell^2_m}
\le\sum_{i=1}^m\sum_{j=1}^n\psi(|s_i-t_j|).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Combining (4.2) and (5.2),
\[
\boxed{
|\operatorname{Cov}(F,G)|
\le C_0\|F-\mathbb EF\|_2\|G-\mathbb EG\|_2
\sum_{i,j}e^{-\lambda|s_i-t_j|},
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
for arbitrary square-integrable functionals \(F=F((X_{s_i})_i)\) and \(G=G((X_{t_j})_j)\). This retains every cross-time bond and is valid for interlaced sets. It does not assert the refuted coefficient-one minimum-distance estimate.

## 6. Specialization to the SU(2) heat semigroup

For normalized Haar measure on \(SU(2)\), the bi-invariant heat kernel has Peter–Weyl expansion
\[
p_t(g)=\sum_{j\in\{0,1/2,1,\ldots\}}
(2j+1)\,\chi_j(g)\,e^{-\kappa j(j+1)t},
\tag{6.1}
\]
where \(\kappa>0\) depends on the metric normalization and \(\chi_0=1\). Since \(|\chi_j(g)|\le2j+1\),
\[
\sup_{g\in SU(2)}|p_t(g)-1|
\le\sum_{j\ge1/2}(2j+1)^2e^{-\kappa j(j+1)t}
\le C_\kappa e^{-3\kappa t/4},
\qquad t\ge1.
\tag{6.2}
\]
The heat kernel is smooth and strictly positive for every \(t>0\). Compactness gives the positive lower and finite upper bounds in (2.4). Thus the hypotheses of Sections 2–5 hold for stationary SU(2) Brownian motion, with a uniform exponential envelope in time.

**Applicability gate:** Before using this in PF-I16, verify that the one-link reference process in the product-process cumulant bridge is precisely the stationary SU(2) heat semigroup (or another compact-state semigroup with the same strict-positivity and uniform-kernel-mixing properties). A mere \(L^2\) spectral-gap bound is not enough to infer (2.3)–(2.4).

## 7. Consequence for PF-I16 and remaining work

If the one-link reference semigroup satisfies Section 6, then every pair of multi-time super-row functionals on a link obeys the cross-cut estimate (5.3). In the four-dimensional plaquette family, a link has at most six incident plaquette rows, so a single two-super-row comparison has at most six time ports on either side and at most 36 cross-time bonds. The resulting finite port factor is harmless at exponential-in-order scale.

This provides a concrete replacement for the failed generic bridge-contraction blueprint:
1. establish strict positivity and uniform heat-kernel convergence for the actual one-link semigroup;
2. use the Doeblin bridge minorization (3.6) to obtain subjective HGR bounds;
3. apply N-against-M tensorization to retain all interlaced temporal cuts;
4. insert this two-block estimate into the multi-row conditional-cumulant recursion and prove that the resulting cut multigraph admits a support-labelled spanning-tree extraction with exponentially controlled multiplicity.
