# PF-I16 — Projective multi-time super-row cumulant tree by bounded link incidence

**Date:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Support-restricted all-orders cumulant tree ESTABLISHED for projective monomial super-rows with bounded total link-port incidence, under the uniform one-link \(L^2\) gap. The arbitrary-bounded-path-functional extension is NOT claimed. The numerical coefficient is explicit as a finite local combinatorial constant but has not been shown small enough for the proposed KP criterion.  
**Scope:** independent stationary link paths; finite row family; each row/super-row has an absolutely convergent projective expansion into products of one-time link observables. No P0–P5 gate is promoted.

## 1. Why this is the right class of super-rows

The bridge-Doeblin estimate controls arbitrary bounded functions of several observation times on one link, but its two-block covariance consequence alone cannot yield a product of \(r-1\) temporal bonds. A common latent variable is the standard obstruction: all pairwise cut covariances can be \(O(\varepsilon)\) while a higher cumulant is also \(O(\varepsilon)\), not \(O(\varepsilon^{r-1})\).

The super-rows generated by the PF-I16 link-conditional cumulant expansion have more structure than arbitrary path functionals. The actual face and face-pair insertions admit absolutely convergent projective expansions into products of one-link, one-time factors. Conditional cumulants, products, and finite sums preserve this *monomial-in-link-ports* structure. A super-row may use a given link at several times, but it does so through a bounded number of explicit one-time factors on that link.

This allows the one-link Markov cumulant estimate to be lifted through the Leonov–Shiryaev formula while keeping the number of slots on each link bounded.

## 2. One-link weighted dependency bound

Let \(X_e(t)\) be a stationary Markov path with invariant law \(\mu_e\) and
\[
\|P_t^e-\Pi_e\|_{L^2_0(\mu_e)\to L^2_0(\mu_e)}
\le e^{-\Delta_0t}.
\tag{2.1}
\]
For bounded \(f_j\) and ordered times \(s_1\le\cdots\le s_q\), the Boolean cumulant operator identity is
\[
B_q(f_1(X_{s_1}),\ldots,f_q(X_{s_q}))
=
\left\langle 1,\,
M_{f_1}(P^e_{s_2-s_1}-\Pi_e)M_{f_2}\cdots
(P^e_{s_q-s_{q-1}}-\Pi_e)M_{f_q}1
\right\rangle_{\mu_e}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Consequently
\[
|B_q|\le
\prod_{j=1}^q\|f_j\|_\infty\,
e^{-\Delta_0(s_q-s_1)}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
This identity is an operator identity and does not require a finite state space; it applies to the compact-group heat-kernel semigroup.

The universal Boolean-to-classical cumulant formula expresses the classical cumulant as
\[
\kappa(Z_1,\ldots,Z_q)
=
\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal P_{\rm irr}[q]}
d_\rho\prod_{C\in\rho}B_{|C|}(Z_j:j\in C),
\tag{2.4}
\]
where \(\mathcal P_{\rm irr}[q]\) is the finite set of irreducible set partitions and \(d_\rho\) are universal coefficients. For an irreducible partition, the sum of the time spans of its blocks is at least \(s_q-s_1\). Thus, with
\[
b_1=1,\qquad
b_q=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal P_{\rm irr}[q]}|d_\rho|
\quad(q\ge2),
\tag{2.5}
\]
we obtain
\[
\boxed{
|\kappa(f_1(X_{s_1}),\ldots,f_q(X_{s_q}))|
\le b_q\prod_j\|f_j\|_\infty\,
e^{-\Delta_0(s_q-s_1)}.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]
For the complete graph on these \(q\) time slots with edge weights
\(w_{ab}=e^{-\Delta_0|s_a-s_b|}\), the maximum spanning-tree weight is exactly \(e^{-\Delta_0(s_q-s_1)}\): the chronological path attains it, and every tree path joining the extreme times has total length at least their separation. Hence (2.6) is a one-link weighted-dependency-graph bound with maximum-tree weight and constants \(b_q\).

The Boolean-cumulant identity and irreducible-partition conversion are the Markov-chain argument of Féray, *Weighted dependency graphs*, Section 10 (arXiv:1605.03836), written in semigroup form. The finite-state assumption in that example is not used in (2.2)–(2.6); only stationarity, the Markov property, and (2.1) are used.

## 3. The bounded-incidence monomial lift

Let \(r\) current rows/super-rows be indexed by \([r]\), and let \(R\) be the number of original insertion labels represented by this family. An elementary monomial row has the form
\[
A_i=\prod_{\alpha\in S_i}Y_\alpha,\qquad
Y_\alpha=f_\alpha(X_{e(\alpha)}(s_\alpha)),
\tag{3.1}
\]
where each slot \(\alpha\) specifies one link, one time, and one bounded one-link function. Different slots in one row may use the same link at different times. Super-rows may contain arbitrarily many slots; the required uniform budget is on the whole family, not on each current row.

Assume:
- the total number of slots is bounded by the original local port budget, \(N:=\sum_i|S_i|\le mR\);
- each link has at most \(D\) slots in the entire current family, counted with multiplicity:
\[
q_e:=|\{\alpha:e(\alpha)=e\}|\le D.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Let \(G_{\rm port}\) be the graph on row labels with
\[
ij\in E(G_{\rm port})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
S_i,S_j\text{ contain slots on at least one common link}.
\]
Give this edge the weight
\[
W_{ij}:=
\max_{\substack{\alpha\in S_i,\ \beta\in S_j\\
e(\alpha)=e(\beta)}}
e^{-\Delta_0|s_\alpha-s_\beta|}.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Set \(W_{ij}=0\) if there is no common link.

Define the finite local partition constants
\[
K_q:=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi([q])}\prod_{B\in\pi}b_{|B|},
\qquad
K_D^*:=\max\left(1,\max_{1\le q\le D}K_q^{1/q}\right),
\qquad
C_{m,D}:=(K_D^*)^m.
\tag{3.4}
\]
These are explicit universal constants once the finite list \(b_1,\ldots,b_D\) is fixed.

**Theorem.** Under (2.1)–(3.2),
\[
\boxed{
|\kappa(A_1,\ldots,A_r)|
\le (K_D^*)^{N}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^r\prod_{\alpha\in S_i}
\|f_\alpha\|_\infty\right)
\mathcal M(G_{\rm port},W)
\le C_{m,D}^{\,R}
\left(\prod_{i,\alpha}\|f_\alpha\|_\infty\right)
\mathcal M(G_{\rm port},W),
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
where \(\mathcal M(G_{\rm port},W)\) is the maximum weight of a spanning tree of the weighted row graph, and is zero when that graph is disconnected.

In particular,
\[
|\kappa(A_1,\ldots,A_r)|
\le C_{m,D}^{\,R}
\left(\prod_{i,\alpha}\|f_\alpha\|_\infty\right)
\sum_{T\in\mathcal T(G_{\rm port})}
\prod_{\{i,j\}\in T}W_{ij}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

### Proof

Apply the Leonov–Shiryaev cumulant-of-products formula:
\[
\kappa(A_1,\ldots,A_r)
=
\sum_{\substack{\pi\in\Pi(\bigsqcup_i S_i)\\
\pi\vee\mathcal I=\hat 1}}
\prod_{B\in\pi}
\kappa(Y_\alpha:\alpha\in B),
\tag{3.7}
\]
where \(\mathcal I=\{S_1,\ldots,S_r\}\) is the partition into row slots. The join condition says that the block-slot incidence hypergraph connects all row labels.

A block \(B\) containing slots from two different links has zero cumulant, because the corresponding link paths are independent. Thus every nonzero term in (3.7) has each block contained in a single link. The partition \(\pi\) therefore factors link-by-link as \(\pi=\bigsqcup_e\pi_e\), with \(\pi_e\in\Pi(q_e)\).

For each block \(B\), apply (2.6). Choose a maximum-weight temporal spanning tree on its slots. The union of these slot trees, after projecting each slot to its row label and deleting loops, is connected: this is exactly the join condition \(\pi\vee\mathcal I=\hat1\). Every projected edge joins two rows with a common link, and its weight is at most the corresponding \(W_{ij}\). A connected graph contains a spanning tree; deleting the other edges can only increase the product because all weights lie in \([0,1]\). It follows that, for each admissible \(\pi\),
\[
\prod_{B\in\pi}
\left(\prod_{\alpha\in B}\|f_\alpha\|_\infty\right)
b_{|B|}\mathcal M_e(B)
\le
\left(\prod_{i,\alpha}\|f_\alpha\|_\infty\right)
\mathcal M(G_{\rm port},W).
\tag{3.8}
\]

It remains to sum the coefficients. Dropping the global join constraint only increases the sum, and the link factorization gives
\[
\sum_{\pi\ {\rm admissible}}\prod_{B\in\pi}b_{|B|}
\le
\prod_e K_{q_e}
\le
(K_D^*)^{\sum_e q_e}
=(K_D^*)^{N}
\le
(K_D^*)^{mR}
=C_{m,D}^{\,R}.
\tag{3.9}
\]
Combining (3.8) and (3.9) proves (3.5). Since the maximum spanning-tree weight is bounded by the sum of all spanning-tree weights, (3.6) follows. \(\square\)

## 4. Projective rows and actual PF-I16 insertions

The theorem extends by multilinearity and absolute convergence to rows with projective expansions
\[
F_i=\int_{\Omega_i}
\prod_{\alpha\in S_i(\omega)}
f_{i,\alpha,\omega}(X_{e_{i,\alpha}}(s_{i,\alpha}))
\,d\nu_i(\omega),
\tag{4.1}
\]
provided
\[
\int_{\Omega_i}\prod_{\alpha\in S_i(\omega)}
\|f_{i,\alpha,\omega}\|_\infty\,d|\nu_i|(\omega)
\le\mathfrak p(F_i),
\tag{4.2}
\]
and the port and per-link incidence bounds hold uniformly on the expansion support. Then
\[
\boxed{
|\kappa(F_1,\ldots,F_r)|
\le C_{m,D}^{\,R}\prod_i\mathfrak p(F_i)
\sum_{T\in\mathcal T(G_{\rm port})}
\prod_{\{i,j\}\in T}
\left(
\sum_{\substack{\alpha\in S_i,\ \beta\in S_j\\
e(\alpha)=e(\beta)}}
e^{-\Delta_0|s_\alpha-s_\beta|}
\right).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
The inner sum dominates \(W_{ij}\), so this is the support-restricted multi-time super-row tree bound needed by the recursive link expansion. In particular, a super-row may contain several time ports on the same link; the theorem does not collapse it to a single time or use a minimum-distance shortcut.

For the recorded PF-I16 insertion inventory:
- a face insertion has at most four link ports and \(\mathfrak p(v_f)\le256b_0\);
- a face-pair insertion has at most eight link ports and \(\mathfrak p(v_{fg})\le15318\,b_0^2/a_0\);
- the conservative incidence inventory gives at most eight face supports and 224 adjacent-face-pair supports per link, hence \(D\le232\) when both insertion types are included.

Thus (4.3) applies to the actual face/pair family with \(m=8,D=232\), under the previously recorded projective-norm and uniform one-link gap hypotheses. For face-only rows one may use \(m=4,D\le8\) (or the sharper geometric incidence if separately verified). The same bound applies at intermediate conditional-cumulant steps: the super-row clusters partition the original labels, so their total number of slots remains \(N\le mR\), and each link's total slot count remains at most \(D\). Each block cumulant supplies an internal tree on its source labels; the outer super-row tree joins these clusters by edges that each come from a pair of original ports on a common link. The union of the internal cluster trees and the outer tree is connected and has exactly \(R-1\) edges, hence is a spanning tree on the original insertion labels. The temporal weight on each edge is the corresponding original port-pair weight. This is the decorated-tree lift that makes the estimate stable under recursive conditional-cumulant decomposition.

## 5. What this closes—and what it does not

- One-link Markov weighted-dependency estimate in maximum-tree form: **ESTABLISHED** under the uniform one-link \(L^2\) gap.
- All-orders support-restricted tree for projective monomial super-rows with bounded total link-port incidence: **ESTABLISHED**, equations (3.5)–(4.3).
- Arbitrary bounded functions of an entire link-path block, with no projective/monomial representation: **NOT COVERED** by this theorem. The bridge-Doeblin group-cut estimate remains the result for that larger class.
- The constant \(C_{8,232}\) is finite and independent of volume, order, and refinement, but its present definition is too crude to claim it meets the numerical KP threshold. A sharp evaluation or a better local resummation of \(K_q\) is still needed.
- Marked-root summation, quantum connected-resolvent/KP convergence, the interacting-vacuum Poincaré inequality, scale-setting along the physical bare-coupling trajectory, and the continuum mass gap remain **OPEN**.

No P0–P5 acceptance status is promoted.

## 7. Recursive-decoration clarification

The one-shot theorem (3.5)–(4.3) is a theorem for the current row labels and their projective monomial expansions. When a current row represents a cluster of original labels, the ordinary projective norm in (4.2) does not itself retain the internal temporal bonds already generated inside that cluster. Consequently, the final paragraph of Section 4 is to be interpreted using the decorated projective norm and transport lemma in `PF_I16_DECORATED_SUPERROW_TREE_TRANSPORT_2026_11_15.md`. That lemma carries the internal tree term-by-term and supplies the original-label spanning-tree conclusion. Without that decoration, only the current-superrow-label tree is asserted. The recursive law-of-total-cumulance implementation has an additional conservative Bell-partition overhead as recorded there; it is not part of the sharper one-shot constant (3.5).
