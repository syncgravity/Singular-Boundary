# PF-I16: canonical reverse-mark transversal reduction for mixed lift expansion

Date: 2026-11-17

## 1. The target-side classification

Use the corrected classification in
PF_I16_TARGET_SIDE_REVERSE_MARK_CLASSIFICATION_2026_11_17.md.
A valid reverse mark J={a,b} in a child target T is only:

- Type I: adjacent endpoints of degrees (1,3) or (2,2);
- Type II: distance-two endpoints, one leaf and one degree-two vertex,
  with one of the two ordered path orientations.

The distance-three leaf/leaf path is invalid because the restored old edge
is already present in the induced local tree.

## 2. A possible way to turn mixed expansion into a transversal

Fix an injective matching Q from parent sources p to canonical parent targets
Q(p). A row is r=(p,J), with demand delta(p,J), and its lift set L(r)
consists of child targets generated from Q(p) by inserting J last.

Every T in L(r) has J as a valid reverse mark and its inverse parent target
is Q(p). If one could choose, for each row r, a submeasure nu_r on L(r)
with total mass delta(p,J), such that
\[
\sum_r \nu_r(T)\le1\quad\text{for every child target }T,
\]
then the desired lift kernel would follow immediately.

A strong sufficient condition is a canonical-mark selector
\[
\mathfrak m(T)\in\mathcal J(T)
\]
with the property that for each row r=(p,J), there is a submeasure of
mass delta(p,J) supported on
\[
L(r)\cap\{T:\mathfrak m(T)=J\}.
\]
Indeed, if two rows r=(p,J) and r'=(p',J') charge the same T, then
J=mathfrak m(T)=J'. Reverse inversion gives the same parent target
Q(p)=P_J(T)=Q(p'), and injectivity of Q gives p=p'. Thus at most one row
charges any T, and its mass is at most one because delta(p,J)<=1.

This would prove the mixed-family Hall inequalities without estimating
arbitrary unions directly. It is a genuine transversal strategy, not a
consequence of reverse-mark uniqueness alone.

## 3. The missing canonical-mark lemma

The needed statement is:

**Canonical-mark lift lemma (open).** There exists a selector m(T) from
the valid reverse marks of every child target T such that, for every parent
source p and every non-root leaf interval J of a child source in the
(p,J)-fiber, the admissible final lifts of Q(p) with J inserted last contain
enough distinct targets T with m(T)=J to carry the entire split demand
delta(p,J).

The required amount is delta(p,J), not merely one target per row. In
particular, a proof must handle all rows in a fixed parent fiber and all
Type-I/Type-II interactions. A lexicographically first valid mark is a
natural candidate, but no argument currently shows that it is attainable
with sufficient multiplicity from every row.

## 4. Why this is sharper than a pointwise mark count

The invalid double-star leaf-pair family is not relevant to the implemented
inverse. The actual possible collisions are generated by Type-I and Type-II
marks. The canonical selector would orient those collisions: every target
is assigned to one valid mark, and parent matching Q then identifies at
most one parent source for that mark.

This separates the problem into two logically distinct parts:

1. Parent-source separation: supplied by injectivity of Q.
2. Mark separation: supplied by the open canonical-mark lift lemma.

The first part is already available at finite parent orders and in the
all-orders canonical-parent matching theorem established in the prior
work. The second part is the new combinatorial obstacle.

## 5. What has and has not been proved

The implication from the canonical-mark lift lemma to the desired
capacity-one kernel is rigorous. The lemma itself is not proved. In
particular, it is not valid to choose the smallest reverse mark after
constructing T and assume every row can realize that choice.

The corrected target-side classification is an established tree-theoretic
reduction. The all-orders mixed lift expansion, global cross-fiber Hall
inequality, and weighted one-link inequality remain open.
