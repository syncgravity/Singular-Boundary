# Star-only local-switch fractional transport: exact obstruction (2026-11-16)

## Target

For each left source x=(M,tau), let C_star(x) be the set of distinct port trees generated by all parent-before-child insertion orders, all admissible overlapping current-tree edges, and only the two middle-centred star replacements. The proposed uniform transport is

\[
\pi_\star(x,T)=|C_\star(x)|^{-1}\,\mathbf1_{T\in C_\star(x)}.
\]

The local-switch cut-load identity is exact for this restricted move set:
\[
T\in C_\star(M,\tau)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c(T)=m(M).
\]
Thus the relation preserves exact load classes. The question tested here is whether every right-tree column has mass at most one.

## Exact exhaustive results

The verifier `research/verification/verify_pf_i16_star_only_fractional_transport.py` enumerates every perfect matching, every spanning tree of its interval-overlap graph, all legal parent-before-child insertion orders, all eligible overlapping edges at each insertion, and both star replacements. Candidate outputs are deduplicated per source. Column masses are accumulated with exact rational arithmetic.

| ports n | left sources (M,tau) | distinct candidate port trees | candidate-set size range | maximum column mass |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 2 | 2 | 2–2 | 1 |
| 6 | 22 | 21 | 4–12 | 3/2 |
| 8 | 608 | 546 | 8–128 | 153/44 |

Therefore the uniform star-only transport **fails already at six ports**. At eight ports its maximum column mass is 153/44, approximately 3.47727. The exact-profile property is valid, but this particular uniform weighting is not a fractional Hall certificate.

For n=6, one maximum-load target tree is
\[
T=\{14,24,34,45,46\},
\]
and its incoming mass is exactly 3/2. For n=8, a maximum-load target is the 7-edge star centred at port 5,
\[
T=\{15,25,35,45,56,57,58\},
\]
with incoming mass 153/44.

## Consequence

This rules out the proposed uniform distribution on the star-only candidate sets as an all-orders proof strategy. It does **not** disprove the exact-class Hall inequality L(h)<=Q(h), nor the weighted one-link inequality. The candidate relation may still support a nonuniform transport: sources with small candidate sets need not distribute mass uniformly, and target-specific reweighting may reduce the overloaded columns. Another possibility is to enlarge the star-only relation with additional profile-preserving moves or couple different insertion histories before assigning mass.

The earlier three-move transport (two stars plus the increasing path) has a different candidate relation and its finite column results do not transfer to this star-only restriction. No all-orders column bound is claimed.
