# PF-I16: all-orders sibling-source geometric diamond

Date: 2026-11-19  
Status: **proved at the labelled port-tree / guarded-reversal level for canonical sibling fibers; accepted-face support confluence and canonical membership at the common parent remain open.**

## 1. Definitions

A source is an ordered matching-tree pair S=(M,τ), with the overlap tree rooted at the matching interval containing port 1. For a non-root leaf interval I, let π_I(S) be the source obtained by deleting I, deleting its leaf vertex from τ, and compressing the remaining port labels in order.

Let C(S) be the set of port trees generated by admissible parent-before-child insertion histories for S. Let C_can(S) be the sub-neighborhood generated by histories in which the canonical non-root leaf ℓ(S) is inserted last.

For a target tree T and a two-port mark J, write P_J(T) for the unique guarded inverse when it exists. The exact fiber neighborhood is
\[
N(S,J)=\{T:\ J\text{ is a valid guarded reverse mark of }T,
\quad P_J(T)\in C_{\mathrm{can}}(S)\}.
\]

The target-side guarded mutual-survival diamond is the theorem recorded in
PF_I16_GUARDED_MUTUAL_SURVIVAL_DIAMOND_LEMMA_2026_11_18.md: if two disjoint valid marks each remain valid after the opposite inverse, their two double inverses agree as labelled port trees.

## 2. Theorem

**Theorem (all-orders canonical-sibling port-tree diamond).**  
Let p,q be sibling sources over a common source r, with distinguished intervals K∈p and J∈q, such that
\[
\pi_K(p)=\pi_J(q)=r,
\qquad
K=\ell(p),\qquad J=\ell(q).
\tag{1}
\]
For any target tree
\[
T\in N(p,J)\cap N(q,K),
\tag{2}
\]
the two guarded inverse orders are defined and agree:
\[
U:=P_K(P_J(T))=P_J(P_K(T)).
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover,
\[
U\in C(r).
\tag{4}
\]
All statements hold for every finite port order; no finite-order census is used in the proof.

### Proof

Put A=P_J(T) and B=P_K(T). By (2),
\[
A\in C_{\mathrm{can}}(p),\qquad B\in C_{\mathrm{can}}(q).
\]

Because K=ℓ(p), every history witnessing A∈C_can(p) inserts K last. Its final insertion is exactly a guarded local switch at K. Therefore K is a valid guarded reverse mark of A, and P_K(A) is the preceding target in that history. In particular,
\[
P_K(A)\in C(\pi_K(p))=C(r).
\tag{5}
\]
The same argument, using J=ℓ(q), shows that J is a valid guarded reverse mark of B, and
\[
P_J(B)\in C(\pi_J(q))=C(r).
\tag{6}
\]

Thus the two original marks J,K are mutually surviving in the exact target-side sense. They are disjoint because they are distinct matching intervals in the sibling source extensions. The guarded mutual-survival diamond theorem applies and gives
\[
P_K(P_J(T))=P_J(P_K(T)).
\]
Combining this equality with (5) and (6) proves (3) and (4). The equality is equality of labelled edge sets on the common remaining ordered port set, so it preserves the complete port-tree incidence data. □

## 3. What this closes

This proves the missing source-critical-pair lifting statement for the canonical-sibling configuration (1): canonical forced-last membership on the two sibling fibers supplies the two survival hypotheses required by the target diamond. No additional assumption about disjoint four-port supports is needed.

It also proves a useful parent-neighborhood conclusion: the common double inverse belongs to the **full** output neighborhood C(r), by either history. Hence the two canonical sibling reductions have a common parent target, and the result is an all-orders port-tree diamond rather than a finite-order pattern.

The proof uses only:
- exact source identity under the two parent projections;
- the repository's forced-last definition of C_can;
- uniqueness and validity of the guarded inverse for a fixed mark; and
- the all-finite-port target-side mutual-survival theorem.

## 4. Explicit nonclaims: the remaining L8 boundary

The theorem does **not** prove
\[
U\in C_{\mathrm{can}}(r).
\tag{C3}
\]
That is a stronger requirement: the common target must admit a history in which the canonical leaf of r is last. The proof above establishes only U∈C(r). The exact reduction already recorded as (C4) in the canonical source-diamond census is:
\[
U\in C_{\mathrm{can}}(r)
\iff
\ell(r)\text{ is a valid guarded reverse mark of }U
\text{ and }
P_{\ell(r)}(U)\in C(r^-),
\]
where r^- is obtained by deleting ℓ(r). Proving those two conditions from the sibling histories is the remaining canonical-parent obligation.

Nor does equality of labelled port trees by itself identify accepted Penrose--Fibonacci face supports. In particular, this theorem does not yet provide:
- an L6c-compatible isomorphism of the complete support/intersection diagrams;
- an L7b proof for the cyclic boundary words and relative-collapse signatures; or
- naturality and coherence of those support isomorphisms under further deletions.

Accordingly, this closes the **canonical-sibling source-to-target port-tree diamond**, not full geometric L8 on accepted rhombus supports, and not the all-orders multivalued-π Hall inequality.

## 5. The canonical strengthening is not available in this generality

The n=10 third-mark census in
PF_I16_ALL_ORDERS_THIRD_MARK_PROOF_ATTEMPT_2026_11_18.md
shows that the stronger conclusion U∈C_can(r) fails in 320,655 common-target incidences. The distinct-parent follow-up also finds 286,440 canonical-parent failures among 765,852 common-target incidences, so excluding p=q does not repair the implication.

One explicit distinct-parent witness has J=(2,3), K=(4,6), with
\[
p=(((1,6),(2,4),(3,7),(5,8)),((0,1),(0,2),(0,3))),
\]
\[
q=(((1,6),(2,3),(4,7),(5,8)),((0,1),(0,2),(0,3))),
\]
and common parent
\[
r=(((1,4),(2,5),(3,6)),((0,1),(0,2))).
\]
The common parent target is
\[
U=((1,2),(2,3),(3,4),(4,5),(4,6)).
\]
It is in C(r), as theorem (4) guarantees, but the canonical leaf A=(2,5) is not a guarded reverse mark of U. Thus U is not in C_can(r).

This does not refute the port-tree diamond (3)-(4): the two inverse orders still agree, and their common result is an ordinary output of r. It does refute any attempt to promote (4) to canonical-parent membership without additional hypotheses.
