## 1. Objective

The November 23 Parent-Fiber Exchange audit identified the fixed-\((P,Q)\) two-mark square as rigid at n=6,8. Its prescribed next step was to allow the second parent projection to move:
\[
Q=P_K(T)\longrightarrow Q'=P_K(T'),
\]
while retaining the first parent projection \(P=P_J(T)=P_J(T')\), the source identity, the two reverse marks, and exact guarded inverse witnesses.

This census implements that test as a witnessed D–S–D path:

1. **D:** at the initial target \(T\), the two marks \(J,K\) are mutually surviving; their two double inverses agree and lie in the ordinary common-parent neighborhood \(C(r)\).
2. **S:** move from \(T\) to \(T'\) inside the same fixed-source, fixed-mark, fixed-parent-target block \(L_J(P)\).
3. **D:** at \(T'\), verify the same mutually-surviving two-mark diamond and ordinary common-parent membership.

The resulting projection transition is \(Q\to Q'\). The graph is keyed by the exact source \(x\), ordered marks \((J,K)\), and fixed first parent target \(P\). No quotient across different child sources is taken.

## 2. Exact definitions and guards

For a source \(x=(M,\tau)\), let \(J,K\) be distinct eligible non-root leaves. Write
\[
p=\pi_J(x),\qquad q=\pi_K(x).
\]
For a retained target \(T\), put
\[
P=P_J(T),\qquad Q=P_K(T).
\]

The retained J-row is restricted by the actual canonical-parent condition:
\[
P_J(T)\in C_{\rm can}(p).
\]
Likewise, K-retention requires \(P_K(T)\in C_{\rm can}(q)\).

Every inverse is checked with the repository's unique fixed-mark inverse and compression routines. The verifier additionally checks:
- the restored edge is absent from the target (no reinsertion);
- the restored edge has positive-length overlap with the marked interval;
- the inverse removes exactly the three local switch edges and restores exactly one old edge;
- the compressed parent target is in the required canonical parent neighborhood.

For a D-edge, both second inverses are recomputed after compressing the opposite mark. The two orders must produce the same double inverse \(U\), and \(U\) must belong to the ordinary common-parent neighborhood \(C(r)\). **Canonical membership \(U\in C_{\rm can}(r)\) is neither required nor asserted.** This respects the all-orders canonical-sibling diamond theorem and its explicit canonical-parent counterexample.

For an S-step \(T\to T'\), both targets are generated by the same source \(x\), the same mark \(J\), and the same projected parent target \(P\). The exact local switch witnesses are retained.

## 4. Interpretation

### 4.1 The fixed-projection obstruction is real

Every tested fixed-\((P,Q)\) group is a singleton. No nontrivial target-changing square preserves both parent projections in the tested source/mark incidences.

### 4.2 Projection-shifting transitions exist

Allowing \(Q\) to change produces 102 witnessed projection shifts at n=6 and 59,860 at n=8. Thus the next exchange mechanism need not preserve both parent projections. The D–S–D paths provide exact, source-preserving witnesses for these shifts.

The SCC census shows that these transitions form nontrivial strongly connected sink classes in most of the tested fixed-\((x,J,K,P)\) graphs. This is a finite structural signal, not a capacity theorem.

### 4.3 What the result does not establish

The current graph keeps the child source \(x\) fixed throughout its S-step. It does **not** yet include S-edges between distinct child sources \(x\ne x'\) in the same fixed fiber \((p,J)\). Consequently it does not resolve the non-diamond collisions emphasized in the Parent-Fiber Exchange audit.

It also does not:
- construct a fractional allocation with fiber row sums \(\delta(F)\) and target loads at most one;
- prove the weighted dual \(MR_k\), blockwise Hall \(BQH_k\), or quotient Hall \(QH\);
- establish the all-orders multivalued-\(\pi\) Hall theorem;
- establish WI;
- assign any accepted \(\mathbb Z^5\) label or helical phase to a reverse mark;
- derive the dissertation pitches \(3/13,5/13,8/13\).

No n=10 projection-shifting census is claimed.

## 6. Next proof task

Extend the two-colour incidence complex across **distinct child sources in the same fiber**.

For a fixed \((p,J,P)\), the S relation may connect
\[
(x,J,T)\quad\text{to}\quad(x',J,T')
\]
with \(x,x'\in X(p,J)\), \(T,T'\in L_J(P)\), and \(x\ne x'\). A D-edge at \(T'\) then uses the eligible marks of \(x'\), not necessarily the same second mark \(K\) as at \(x\).

This is the missing layer for non-diamond collisions: two fibers may share a target through different source witnesses, so there is no same-source D-edge connecting those incidences. The next exact census should:
1. construct the full source-mark-target incidence vertices without quotienting away \(x\);
2. add all S-edges within each fixed \((p,J,P)\) block, including cross-source edges;
3. add only exact same-source mutually-surviving D-edges;
4. analyze alternating components and sink classes while retaining source demand \(1/d(x)\);
5. identify whether any closed component is actually deficient, or whether its S-block structure supplies spare target capacity.

Only after this cross-source structure is understood should a target-capacity allocation be designed. The helical map remains a later geometric refinement: a phase must be derived from an authenticated source-to-accepted-\(\mathbb Z^5\) incidence map, not assigned to these graph vertices by hand.
