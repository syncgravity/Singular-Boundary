# PF-I16 — Conditional HGR does not imply an all-orders cumulant tree

**Date:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Rigorous obstruction to the proposed abstract implication. This does not refute a stronger theorem using the full stationary-Markov/bridge structure; it proves that Peyre N-against-M tensorization alone cannot supply the missing cumulant-tree estimate.

## 1. The proposed implication

The proposed local step was to derive, from pairwise subjective/conditional maximal-correlation bounds,
\[
\rho_{\rm subj}(\sigma(G_a),\sigma(G_b))\le \eta_{ab},
\]
an all-orders estimate
\[
|\kappa(G_1,\ldots,G_q)|
\le C_q\prod_a\|G_a\|_\infty
\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_q}\prod_{\{a,b\}\in T}\eta_{ab},
\tag{1.1}
\]
with a finite constant depending only on q.

Peyre's N-against-M theorem is a pair-of-sigma-fields theorem: it controls the HGR coefficient between two generated sigma-fields from the matrix of pairwise subjective HGR coefficients. It does not state a connected-cumulant estimate. The following three-variable law shows that no such estimate follows from those pairwise hypotheses alone.

## 2. Three-variable hyperedge counterexample

Let X,Y,Z take values in {−1,+1}, with joint law
\[
\mathbb P_\lambda(X=x,Y=y,Z=z)
=\frac18(1+\lambda xyz),\qquad 0<|\lambda|<1.
\tag{2.1}
\]
This is a probability law because all eight masses are positive and sum to one.

**Marginals.** Each variable is centered and uniform. Every two-variable marginal is uniform on {−1,+1}², hence every pair is independent:
\[
\mathbb E X=\mathbb E Y=\mathbb E Z=0,\qquad
\mathbb E XY=\mathbb E XZ=\mathbb E YZ=0.
\tag{2.2}
\]
But
\[
\mathbb E XYZ=\lambda.
\tag{2.3}
\]
Consequently the third joint cumulant is
\[
\boxed{\kappa(X,Y,Z)=\lambda.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

**All-other-coordinate conditional HGR.** Conditional on Z=z,
\[
\mathbb P(X=x,Y=y\mid Z=z)
=\frac14(1+\lambda zxy).
\tag{2.5}
\]
Both conditional marginals remain uniform and the conditional Pearson correlation is \(\lambda z\). For two non-degenerate binary variables, HGR maximal correlation equals the absolute Pearson correlation (the centered L² spaces are one-dimensional). Thus
\[
\rho_{\rm HGR}(X,Y\mid Z=z)=|\lambda|
\quad\text{for every }z.
\tag{2.6}
\]
The same calculation applies cyclically, so
\[
\boxed{
\rho_{\rm subj}(X,Y\mid\sigma(Z))
=\rho_{\rm subj}(X,Z\mid\sigma(Y))
=\rho_{\rm subj}(Y,Z\mid\sigma(X))
=|\lambda|.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]
For three variables, the conditioning variable is exactly the sigma-field generated by all the other coordinates. Therefore this example satisfies the strongest natural pairwise subjective-HGR premise used in the proposed route.

If (1.1) held for q=3 with a finite constant C₃, take \(\eta_{12}=\eta_{13}=\eta_{23}=|\lambda|\). There are exactly three labelled spanning trees on three vertices, and each tree has two edges. Since all three variables have sup norm one, (1.1) would imply
\[
|\lambda|\le 3C_3|\lambda|^2.
\tag{2.8}
\]
For any proposed finite C₃, choosing
\[
0<|\lambda|<\min\{1,(3C_3)^{-1}\}
\]
contradicts (2.8). Hence:

\[
\boxed{\text{No finite }C_3\text{ makes pairwise subjective HGR imply the product-edge cumulant tree.}}
\]

The failure is the order of smallness: a genuine three-way interaction has size O(λ), while every pairwise conditional HGR coefficient is O(λ), so a two-edge tree product is only O(λ²).

## 3. What this does and does not establish

1. **Refuted:** the deduction “pairwise subjective HGR bounds + Peyre N-against-M tensorization imply an all-orders cumulant tree” is invalid in general.
2. **Not refuted:** a cumulant-tree theorem for a single stationary compact-group heat-kernel path may still hold by using additional Markov, bridge, semigroup, or ordered-time structure that the three-variable law (2.1) does not possess.
3. **Required repair:** any successful proof of the one-link block-functional estimate must use a genuinely higher-order Markov-path identity (for example, a controlled multi-cut/iterated conditional cumulant decomposition), not only the pairwise HGR matrix estimate.
4. **Partition-lattice resummation:** this counterexample does not settle the separate occurrence-level combinatorial lemma. It does mean that the proposed proof pipeline cannot invoke (3.4) as a consequence of pairwise HGR. The clustered partition sum remains unproved and must be handled independently or after a stronger local theorem is established.

## 4. Consequence for the proof ledger

The Peyre tensorization result remains a valid pairwise block-decorrelation input under its hypotheses. Its role is restricted to pairwise sigma-field control. It must not be cited as support for a connected-cumulant tree, a KP bound, or an all-orders Feynman–Kac resummation.

No new theorem gate is promoted. The sparse-support, unrestricted-repetition case remains open, as do KP convergence, interacting-vacuum Poincaré, scale setting, and the continuum mass gap.
