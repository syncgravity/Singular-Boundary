# PF-I16 — Conditional resolvent amplitude from pointwise derivative flow

**Date:** 2026-11-13  
**Status:** exact finite-product conditional-oscillation lemma, conditional on the PF-I16 pointwise derivative-flow estimate. The conditional mean is shown to be a boundary-local observable with an explicit derivative bound. This does not yet prove the three-support connected tree estimate.

## 1. Setup

Let
\[
d\nu_\Phi=Z^{-1}e^{2\Phi}dU,\qquad
\Phi=\sum_{f\in F}s_f(H_f),
\]
on a finite accepted-link product \(M=\prod_{e\in E}\mathrm{SU}(2)_e\). Let \(L_\Phi\) be the positive reversible generator and \(G=L_\Phi^{-1}Q\) its centered inverse. Assume the pointwise derivative-flow estimate
\[
\|D_eGf\|_\infty
\le \lambda^{-1}\sum_{b\in E}
e^{-\mu d(e,b)}\|D_bf\|_\infty,
\qquad \mu=\log25,
\tag{1.1}
\]
for centered smooth \(f\). This is the corrected PF-I16 estimate only conditionally on its face-pullback and finite-product Kato comparison inputs; no unconditional physical theorem is asserted here.

Let \(r_k\) be a centered residual supported on \(S_k\), with
\[
A_{k,b}=\|D_br_k\|_\infty,\qquad
M_k=\sum_{b\in S_k}A_{k,b},\qquad
u_k=Gr_k.
\]
Let \(X=S_j\), where \(|X|\le25\), and let \(\mathcal F_{X^c}\) be the sigma-field generated by the links outside \(X\).

## 2. Conditional fluctuation lemma

For fixed outside configuration \(y=U_{X^c}\), the conditional expectation
\(m_k(y)=\mathbb E_{\nu_\Phi}[u_k\mid\mathcal F_{X^c}](y)\)
is an average over the inside variables. Thus
\[
|u_k(U)-m_k(y)|
\le \operatorname{osc}_{X}(u_k),
\]
where the oscillation is over configurations agreeing outside \(X\).

The radius-two \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) link has geodesic diameter \(2\pi\). Changing the links of \(X\) one at a time gives
\[
\operatorname{osc}_{X}(u_k)
\le2\pi\sum_{e\in X}\|D_eu_k\|_\infty.
\]
Using (1.1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|u_k-m_k\|_\infty
&\le\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
\sum_{e\in X}\sum_{b\in S_k}
e^{-\mu d(e,b)}A_{k,b}\\
&\le\frac{2\pi|X|}{\lambda}
M_k e^{-\mu d(X,S_k)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\|Gr_k-\mathbb E[Gr_k\mid\mathcal F_{S_j^c}]\|_{L^p(\nu_\Phi)}
\le\frac{50\pi}{\lambda}
M_k e^{-(\log25)d(S_j,S_k)}
\quad(1\le p\le\infty).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
This is stronger than the proposed conditional \(L^4\) estimate. It requires no conditional Poincaré inequality, Sobolev inequality, or conditional mixing theorem. The only analytic input is (1.1), plus the support-size and link-diameter facts.

## 3. Exact structure of the conditional mean

The conditional law of the variables in \(X\), given \(y=U_{X^c}\), has density
\[
d\nu_\Phi(U_X\mid y)
=Z_X(y)^{-1}
\exp\!\left(2\Phi_X(U_X;y)\right)dU_X,
\]
where
\[
\Phi_X(U_X;y)
=\sum_{f:\,\partial f\cap X\ne\varnothing}
s_f(H_f(U_X,y)).
\]
Terms whose faces do not meet \(X\) cancel from the conditional density. It follows that
\[
q_j(y):=\mathbb E_{\nu_\Phi}[r_j\mid\mathcal F_{X^c}](y)
\]
depends on the outside configuration only through the boundary-link set
\[
B_X=\bigcup_{f:\,\partial f\cap X\ne\varnothing}
(\partial f\setminus X).
\]
In particular, \(q_j\) is a local observable on \(B_X\), not an arbitrary function of all outside links.

There are at most \(8|X|\) faces meeting \(X\), since each link is incident to at most eight accepted faces. Each such face contributes at most three outside links, so
\[
|B_X|\le24|X|\le600.
\]
Differentiating the conditional expectation under the integral gives the exact score-covariance identity
\[
D_zq_j
=2\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_\Phi(\cdot\mid y)}
\left(r_j,D_z\Phi_X\right),
\qquad z\in B_X.
\tag{3.1}
\]
For \(z\notin B_X\), \(D_zq_j=0\). If
\(\|D_zs_f(H_f)\|_\infty\le2\beta_f\) and
\(\beta_*:=\sup_f\beta_f\), then at most eight faces contribute to a fixed \(z\), so
\[
\|D_z\Phi_X\|_\infty\le16\beta_*.
\]
Using
\(|\operatorname{Cov}(A,B)|\le2\|A\|_\infty\|B\|_\infty\)
in (3.1) yields
\[
\boxed{
\|D_zq_j\|_\infty\le64B_j\beta_*,
\qquad
\sum_{z\in B_X}\|D_zq_j\|_\infty
\le38400B_j\beta_*,
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
where \(B_j=\|r_j\|_\infty\). With the recorded allocation
\(\beta_*\le2b_0/a_0\), the last bound is at most
\(76800B_jb_0/a_0\). These are finite-graph bounds independent of total graph volume.

## 4. The remaining boundary-mean term

Write
\[
u_k=(u_k-m_k)+m_k.
\]
The first part is now exponentially small near \(S_j\) by (2.1). But
\[
r_ju_k=r_j(u_k-m_k)+r_jm_k,
\qquad
\mathbb E[r_jm_k\mid\mathcal F_{S_j^c}]
=m_kq_j.
\]
Global centering \(\nu_\Phi(r_j)=0\) does not imply \(q_j=0\). Thus replacing \(u_k\) by its conditional fluctuation in the nested third-order term would silently discard the term involving \(m_kq_j\), and is not valid.

The new information is that \(q_j\) is supported on the finite boundary set \(B_X\) and has the derivative bound (3.2). The remaining task is to control its coupling to the conditional mean \(m_k\), while retaining the outer resolvent-gradient factor and the six-permutation structure. A two-point estimate for a local observable and \(Gr_k\) alone does not immediately control that full weighted boundary term.

## 5. Verdict

**Proved as a finite-product lemma, conditional on (1.1):** the conditional fluctuation of \(Gr_k\) across any residual support of size at most 25 has exponential \(L^\infty\), hence \(L^4\), decay with the full derivative-flow exponent \(\log25\).

**Also derived exactly:** the conditional mean of a residual is a boundary-local observable, with the score-covariance identity (3.1) and the uniform derivative bound (3.2).

**Still open:** the boundary-mean contribution in the nested third-order coefficient and the history-aware six-permutation cancellation needed for a connected three-support tree estimate. The conditional fluctuation lemma by itself does not close that gap.

No conditional Poincaré estimate is claimed or needed for (2.1). No R3 constants are imported. No physical spectral gap, all-orders activity bound, continuum limit, or Yang–Mills mass gap is inferred.


## 6. Exact reduction of the nested third-order term

The boundary contribution can be isolated more sharply by using self-adjointness before differentiating. For centered \(r_i,r_j,r_k\), put \(u_\ell=Gr_\ell\), fix \(X=S_j\), and write
\[
m_\ell=\mathbb E[u_\ell\mid\mathcal F_{X^c}],
\qquad q_j=\mathbb E[r_j\mid\mathcal F_{X^c}].
\]
Since \(G\) is self-adjoint and \(u_i\) is centered,
\[
T_{ijk}:=\nu_\Phi[r_iGQ(r_ju_k)]
=\nu_\Phi[u_i r_j u_k].
\tag{6.1}
\]
Conditioning first on \(X^c\), and defining
\[
c_{ij}:=\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_\Phi(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_{X^c})}
(u_i,r_j),
\]
gives the exact decomposition
\[
\boxed{
T_{ijk}
=\nu_\Phi[u_i r_j(u_k-m_k)]
+\nu_\Phi[m_kc_{ij}]
+\nu_\Phi[q_jm_im_k].
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Indeed, \(\mathbb E[u_ir_j\mid X^c]=m_iq_j+c_{ij}\).

The first two terms have explicit pair-arm decay. The conditional covariance inequality
\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(A,B)|\le
\tfrac14\operatorname{osc}(A)\operatorname{osc}(B)
\]
and \(\operatorname{osc}_X(r_j)\le2B_j\) imply
\[
\|c_{ij}\|_\infty
\le\frac{B_j}{2}\operatorname{osc}_X(u_i)
\le\frac{25\pi}{\lambda}B_jM_i
e^{-(\log25)d(X,S_i)}.
\]
Using also
\(\|u_\ell\|_\infty\le(26\pi/\lambda)M_\ell\)
and (2.1), we obtain
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
|T_{ijk}-\nu_\Phi[q_jm_im_k]|
\le{}&
\frac{1300\pi^2}{\lambda^2}
B_jM_iM_k e^{-(\log25)d(S_j,S_k)}\\
&+\frac{650\pi^2}{\lambda^2}
B_jM_iM_k e^{-(\log25)d(S_j,S_i)}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.3}
\]
All constants are independent of graph volume, conditional on (1.1).

When the six permutations are summed, the remaining boundary terms pair exactly under \(i\leftrightarrow k\):
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{(i,j,k)\in S_3}T_{ijk}
=
2\sum_{j=1}^3
\nu_\Phi[q_jm_im_k]
+\mathcal R_{123},
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
where \(\{i,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{j\}\), and \(\mathcal R_{123}\) is bounded by summing (6.3). In particular,
\[
|\mathcal R_{123}|
\le\frac{1950\pi^2}{\lambda^2}
\sum_{j=1}^3B_jM_iM_k
\sum_{\ell\ne j}e^{-(\log25)d(S_j,S_\ell)}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

This is a sharper diagnosis of the remaining tree problem. The non-decaying part is a symmetric conditional three-observable correlation \(\nu_\Phi[q_jm_im_k]\), where \(q_j\) is local on the boundary of \(S_j\), and \(m_i,m_k\) are conditional resolvent means. The fluctuation and conditional-covariance pieces are no longer obstructions.

**Remaining task:** prove a connected, spatially decaying bound for the six-permutation sum of \(\nu_\Phi[q_jm_im_k]\), using the boundary-local score structure of \(q_j\) and the derivative profiles of the conditional means. No such bound is claimed here.


## 7. Conditional-mean derivative profile and cumulant interpretation

For \(z\in X^c\), differentiating the conditional integral defining \(m_\ell\) gives
\[
D_zm_\ell
=\mathbb E[D_zu_\ell\mid\mathcal F_{X^c}]
+2\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_\Phi(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_{X^c})}
(u_\ell,D_z\Phi_X).
\tag{7.1}
\]
The covariance term vanishes for \(z\notin B_X\). For \(z\in B_X\), the range-covariance inequality and (2.1) give
\[
\left|2\operatorname{Cov}(u_\ell,D_z\Phi_X)\right|
\le \operatorname{osc}_X(u_\ell)\,
\|D_z\Phi_X\|_\infty
\le\frac{800\pi\beta_*}{\lambda}
M_\ell e^{-(\log25)d(X,S_\ell)}.
\]
Consequently the conditional mean has the explicit gradient profile
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\|D_zm_\ell\|_\infty
\le{}&\frac1\lambda\sum_{b\in S_\ell}
e^{-(\log25)d(z,b)}A_{\ell,b}\\
&+\mathbf1_{z\in B_X}
\frac{800\pi\beta_*}{\lambda}
M_\ell e^{-(\log25)d(X,S_\ell)}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{7.2}
\]
Thus its direct derivative tail is centered at \(S_\ell\); the extra conditional-law response is confined to \(B_X\) and carries an exponential factor from \(X\) to \(S_\ell\). This identity is exact on each finite product; the displayed bound is conditional on (1.1) and the isolated-face first-derivative bound.

Finally, \(\nu_\Phi(q_j)=\nu_\Phi(m_i)=\nu_\Phi(m_k)=0\), since conditional expectation preserves means and each \(r_\ell\) is centered. Therefore
\[
\nu_\Phi[q_jm_im_k]
=\operatorname{cum}_{3,\nu_\Phi}(q_j,m_i,m_k),
\]
the third joint cumulant of three centered observables. The six-permutation residual (6.4) is exactly twice the sum of these cumulants, one for each middle support. The remaining theorem can therefore be stated cleanly: prove a spatial tree bound for these conditional third cumulants, using the boundary support of \(q_j\) and the derivative profiles (7.2). The derivative profiles alone do not imply such a cumulant inequality; no tree estimate is claimed.
