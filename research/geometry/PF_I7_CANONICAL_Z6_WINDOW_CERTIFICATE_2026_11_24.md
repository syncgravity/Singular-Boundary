## Independent finite certificate

The new verifier
`research/scripts/verify_pf_i7_canonical_z6_window.py`
reconstructs the source basis from its literal entries, projects all 64 corners of the centred six-cube, computes the convex hull, independently builds the 15-axis H-representation, and reproduces the historical six-slab polytope.

The expected assertions are:

| Quantity | Projected-cube window | Historical six-slab test |
|---|---:|---:|
| Supporting planes | 30 | 12 |
| Facets | 30 rhombi | 12 pentagons |
| Vertices | 32 | 20 |
| Edges | 60 | 30 |
| Generator/facet-normal axes | 6 generators; 15 facet-normal axes | 6 slab-normal axes |

The 64 projected cube corners are not all extreme points: the convex hull has 32 vertices. Every one of the 30 facets has four vertices and equal side lengths. The verifier checks these facts numerically to tight floating-point tolerances and checks the analytic H-representation against the convex-hull planes.

## Interpretation and scope

This resolves the geometric mismatch **conditional on the standard centred-unit-cube cut-and-project convention**. It does not change the physical/internal basis, the (mathbb Z^6) lattice, or the source's Fibonacci edge rule.

The old six-slab test is not a numerical approximation to the same window: it has a different facet structure and a different scale. The old (R_W) is therefore not a canonical scale for the projected-cube window. It was a density calibration for the six-slab model.

The canonical-window correction changes which lattice points are accepted. Therefore the old accepted vertex set, cropped graph, edge counts, eigenvalues, heat traces, and scaling results cannot be carried over as if unchanged. They must be regenerated from the corrected membership test. The verifier deliberately does **not** silently regenerate or overwrite the historical archive.

If the intended model instead uses a non-cubic six-dimensional acceptance cell or a translated/phason-shifted window, that convention must be stated explicitly; it is not specified by the inspected source. Under the ordinary projected-cube convention, the rhombic triacontahedron is the canonical result.
