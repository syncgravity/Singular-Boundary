# R3: full-Euclidean-measure conditional cumulants along a fixed 2+1D anisotropic trajectory

**October 26, 2026 | Classification: finite-regulator theorem, not a quantum mass gap.**

## A. Geometry, Wilson measure and trajectory

Let \(\Lambda_h\) be a finite 2+1-dimensional spacetime cell complex formed from oriented spatial square or Penrose rhombi of edge scale \(h\), crossed with Euclidean time of spacing \(a_t=\kappa h\), with \(0<\kappa_-\le\kappa\le\kappa_+<\infty\). Each spacetime plaquette has four oriented links. Denote link count by \(N_L\) and plaquette count by \(N_P\). Assume geometric bounded incidence and \(N_P\le D N_L\), and whenever an average over a chosen family \(\mathcal B\) of adjacent spatial plaquette pairs is taken, \(N_L/|\mathcal B|\le D_{\mathcal B}\) and every face occurs in at most \(d_{\mathcal B}\) chosen pairs. The constants depend only on bounded incidence/geometry, not on \(h\) or spatial volume. There is no assertion that the literal Stage 26 geometric prescription has been identified with this analytic trajectory.

Fix a physical \(g_3^2>0\), and write the anisotropic Wilson couplings exactly in the form
\[
\beta_p(h)=\lambda_h c_p(h),\qquad \lambda_h=(g_3^2 h)^{-1},\qquad0<c_-\le c_p(h)\le c_+<\infty.
\]
For example, the tree-level geometric spatial choice \(\beta_s(f)=C_s a_t/(g_3^2 A_f)\) with \(A_f=h^2\sigma_f\) and \(\sigma_f\in\{\sin36^\circ,\sin72^\circ\}\) gives \(c_s(f)=C_s\kappa/\sigma_f\). A geometric temporal choice \(\beta_t(e)=C_t w_e/(g_3^2 a_t)\) with \(w_e\) bounded above and below gives \(c_t(e)=C_tw_e/\kappa\). Such a bounded anisotropy trajectory includes \(a_t\asymp h\), but **not** the separately taken Hamiltonian limit \(a_t/h\to0\) without further arguments.

Define the complete spacetime Euclidean Wilson probability on all SU(2) links, including temporal links,
\[
d\mu_{h,\lambda}(U)=Z_h(\lambda)^{-1}\exp[-\lambda X_h(U)]\prod_{\ell\in L}dU_\ell,\quad X_h=\sum_{p\in P}c_p(h)(1-p_p),\quad p_p=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}W_p\in[-1,1].
\]
This is **not** the Hamiltonian ground-state \(|\Omega_h|^2dU\), and the two may not be identified.

## B. Theorem 1: uniform-in-volume mean defect estimate at weak lattice coupling

There is a constant \(C\) depending only on \(c_+,D\), SU(2) Haar geometry and maximal plaquette length four, such that for every \(\lambda\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{\frac1{N_P}\sum_{p\in P}\mathbb E_{\mu_{h,\lambda}}[1-p_p]
\le \frac{C}{c_-}\frac{N_L}{N_P}\frac{1+\log\lambda}{\lambda}.}\tag{B1}
\]
In particular, if \(N_L/N_P\le D'\) uniformly, the right side is \(O((1+\log\lambda)/\lambda)\), independently of the number of spacetime cells. Along \(\lambda_h=1/(g_3^2h)\) it is \(O(g_3^2h[1+\log(1/(g_3^2h))])\) for \(g_3^2h\le1\).

**Proof.** \(F_h(\lambda)=-\log Z_h(\lambda)\) is nonnegative, nondecreasing and concave (\(F_h''=-\operatorname{Var}(X_h)\le0\)); \(F_h(0)=0\). Thus \(\mathbb E X_h=F'_h(\lambda)\le F_h(\lambda)/\lambda\). Fix \(\delta_0>0\) small and let \(\delta=\delta_0/\sqrt\lambda\). On the event that every link lies within SU(2) geodesic distance \(\delta\) of the identity, all four-link plaquette holonomies lie within \(4\delta\) and \(1-p_p\le8\delta^2\). Each link Haar ball has mass at least \(c_H\delta^3\), uniformly for \(\delta\le\delta_0\). Therefore
\[
Z_h(\lambda)\ge (c_H\delta_0^3\lambda^{-3/2})^{N_L}\exp[-8c_+\delta_0^2N_P],
\]
and
\[
F_h(\lambda)\le \tfrac32 N_L\log\lambda+C_H N_L+8c_+\delta_0^2N_P\le C N_L(1+\log\lambda).
\]
Since \(X_h\ge c_-\sum_p(1-p_p)\), (B1) follows. No cluster decomposition, positivity correlation inequality, or gap enters. QED.

If the spacetime lattice has bounded ratio \(N_L/N_P\), one gets an average over *all* spatial and temporal plaquettes. For spatial-only averages, use the bounded ratio of total plaquette count to spatial plaquette count. This proof **does not** give a pointwise estimate for each selected face or its worst exterior condition.

## C. Theorem 2: full-measure, exterior-averaged connected covariance estimate

Let \(f,g\) be two spatial plaquettes at the same time. Let \(\mathcal E_{fg}\) be the sigma algebra generated by links *outside* the union of their link supports. Thus all their links fluctuate jointly under the complete three-dimensional Wilson measure; neighboring temporal Wilson interactions are fully present. Write
\[
C_{fg}(U_{\rm ext})=\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_h}(p_f,p_g\mid\mathcal E_{fg}),\quad d_f=\mathbb E_{\mu_h}(1-p_f).
\]
Then the **exact nonperturbative bound**
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_{\mu_h}|C_{fg}|\le 2\sqrt{d_fd_g}\le d_f+d_g}\tag{C1}
\]
holds. Indeed conditional Cauchy-Schwarz followed by Cauchy-Schwarz over exterior conditions gives \(\mathbb E|C|\le\sqrt{\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(p_f\mid\mathcal E)\,\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(p_g\mid\mathcal E)}\). The law of total variance bounds these by \(\operatorname{Var}(p_f)\), \(\operatorname{Var}(p_g)\). Finally, \(\operatorname{Var}(p_f)\le\mathbb E(p_f-1)^2\le2d_f\), because \(p_f\in[-1,1]\). This argument controls the **actual exterior-modified connected response**, not the isolated-block \(\partial_t\partial_s\log Z_{t,s}(V)\).

For a block-pair collection \(\mathcal B_h\), suppose each face appears in at most \(d_{\mathcal B}\) pairs and \(N_L/|\mathcal B_h|\le D_{\mathcal B}\). Summation of (C1), Theorem 1 and bounded incidence yields
\[
\boxed{\frac1{|\mathcal B_h|}\sum_{(f,g)\in\mathcal B_h}\mathbb E_{\mu_h}|C_{fg}|
\le \frac{C d_{\mathcal B}D_{\mathcal B}}{c_-}\frac{1+\log\lambda_h}{\lambda_h}.}\tag{C2}
\]
This bound is independent of the physical spatial volume and tends to zero along \(g_3^2h\to0\) with \(a_t/h\) bounded away from zero and infinity. **It is a spatially averaged estimate, not a supremum over block locations**. It does not imply an operator-norm bound on the global covariance/influence matrix.

## D. Fixed-spin and graded multilocal extension

Let \(a_j(U)=\chi_j(U)/(2j+1)\in[-1,1]\), for SU(2) spin \(j\). The elementary character decomposition \(a_j(\theta)=(2j+1)^{-1}\sum_{m=-j}^j\cos(2m\theta)\) and \(1-\cos(k\theta)\le k^2(1-\cos\theta)\) for integer \(k\) imply the pointwise estimate
\[
\boxed{1-a_j(U)\le L_j(1-p(U)),\qquad L_j=\frac43j(j+1).}\tag{D1}
\]
Consequently for two spins
\[
\mathbb E|\operatorname{Cov}(a_j(W_f),a_k(W_g)\mid\mathcal E_{fg})|
\le2\sqrt{L_jL_k d_fd_g}.\tag{D2}
\]
For \(q\) normalized elementary spin-character factors \(A=\prod_{i=1}^q a_{j_i}(W_{f_i})\), including noncontiguous faces, \(|A|\le1\) and
\[
1-A\le\sum_{i=1}^q (1-a_{j_i}(W_{f_i})),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(A)\le2\sum_{i=1}^q L_{j_i}d_{f_i}.\tag{D3}
\]
Thus for a bounded-degree, bounded-spin collection of graded multilocal sources with bounded face-incidence per source and uniform source density, an averaged connected-covariance estimate with the same \(O((1+\log\lambda)/\lambda)\) rate follows by the same total-variance argument. This does not cover sources with \(q\) or \(j\) diverging as \(h\to0\); it does not cover all Wilson traces around long non-elementary loops merely by calling them a fixed-grade product.

## E. Why the result is not an R3/R2 mass gap

1. **Euclidean vs Hamiltonian:** The bounds control the *full finite-time Wilson Gibbs measure*, not the transformed Hamiltonian ground-state conditional law. Passing between them uniformly requires a certified positive transfer matrix and controlled \(a_t\to0\) and large-time limits.
2. **Spatial averages vs worst-state operator norm:** (C2) is a trace-like spatial average. A rare block or collective low-energy state may evade it. It does not establish \(\|P_sSP_s\|_{\rm op}\le Cs^p\).
3. **Vanishing unrenormalized source variance:** (D3) shows that bounded-spin elementary Wilson observables become increasingly concentrated near identity in the mean. Their normalized connected covariances may not contract after field renormalization, and their lower observability is not established.
4. **Anisotropy:** This proof requires \(a_t/h\in[\kappa_-,\kappa_+]\). Taking \(a_t/h\to0\) separately changes the coefficient bounds \(c_\pm\), so the current constants do not cover the Hamiltonian anisotropic limit.
5. **Renormalization and Penrose geometry:** Finite face types and bounded valence ensure geometric constants, but Paper 2/3/4's cut-and-project, \(C_5\), \(J\)-operator or one-loop spectral ratios provide none of the missing dynamical inequalities.

The result is a rigorous finite-regulator *typical location* conditional cumulant theorem at a fixed anisotropic continuum trajectory, not a proof of IR spectral decay at a fixed physical scale, of an interacting uniformly observable Wilson frame, or of the pure SU(2) Yang–Mills continuum mass gap.
