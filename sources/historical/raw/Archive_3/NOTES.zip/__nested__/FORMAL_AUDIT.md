## 1. Exact statements from Paper 3
The paper decomposes `R^5 = R_triv ⊕ R_parallel^2 ⊕ R_perp^2`, then fixes the trivial factor and identifies `R_eff^4 ≅ C_(z1) ⊕ C_(z2)`, where `z1=Σ m_j ω^j`, `z2=Σ m_j ω^(2j)`, `ω=e^(2πi/5)` (§2.1). It defines `J` block-diagonally as the ordinary 90-degree real rotation in each of the `(x,y)` and `(u,v)` planes. It establishes `J²=-I`, vanishing Nijenhuis tensor and C5-equivariance: `σ(z1,z2)=(ω z1,ω² z2)` (§2.2). Those are valid linear-algebraic statements on the stipulated effective real vector space. The source then *identifies* `v=ict` and claims that `exp(πJ/2)` performs Wick rotation (§2.3); that physical interpretation requires a separate theorem. In §7 the source specifies `z2(τ)=A exp(2π i pτ)`, with `p=3/13,5/13,8/13`, inspired by Conway-worm commensurability; Remark 6 explicitly calls the time-varying observation of a static perpendicular-space field **kinematic, not dynamical**. It also proposes KK-like mass and 3:5:8 frequency interpretations without a specified Yang–Mills field equation, Hilbert-space representation, or spectral intertwiner.

## 2. Proposition J1 (geometric complex structure, PROVED)
On `R_eff^4 ≅ C²`, let `J(z1,z2)=(iz1,iz2)`. Then `J²=-I`, `exp(θJ)` is an orthogonal one-parameter group, and `J` commutes with the C5 action `(ω,ω²)`. The coefficients are constant and its Nijenhuis tensor vanishes, so this defines the standard integrable complex structure. Proof: elementary multiplication by `i`; C5 action is diagonal complex-linear. **Scope:** geometry only; neither gauge dynamics nor a representation on the physical gauge Hilbert space follows.

## 3. Counterexample J2 (90-degree rotation is not Wick analytic continuation)
On real coordinates `(u,v)`, `exp(πJ/2)(u,v)=(-v,u)` is a real, norm-preserving rotation. Wick continuation `t↦-iτ` changes real to imaginary time and is generally unbounded/unavailable on arbitrary functions without spectrum or analyticity hypotheses. For a one-level Hamiltonian `H=E>0`, `e^{-iHt}` is unitary whereas `e^{-Hτ}` is a strict contraction for τ>0; a mere real orthogonal coordinate rotation does not identify these evolutions. Setting `v=ict` by definition does not prove reflection positivity, analytic continuation, or a physical spacetime metric. Hence the strong claim in §2.3 is an interpretation conditional on additional physical identifications, not an established interacting-gauge Wick-rotation theorem.

## 4. Proposition H1 (helical observation is kinematic, PROVED)
Let `F:C→R` be a static measurable field and `h_p(τ)=A e^{2πipτ}`. Then `f_p(τ)=F(h_p(τ))` is a valid pullback of a static field and can oscillate without `F` having any dynamical evolution. For `F(re^{iθ})=cos(mθ)`, `f_p(τ)=cos(m arg A+2πmpτ)`. Thus sampling frequencies `mp` are exactly kinematic. There is no implication that they lie in the spectrum of any interacting Hamiltonian.

Counterexample H2: take a gauge theory with a fixed self-adjoint transfer Hamiltonian `H`, and choose two external windows whose geometric pitches differ. The windows can have arbitrarily different sampling frequencies while `spec(H)` is unchanged. Thus no operator-independent mass formula `E_p ∝ p` follows. A time-dependent observer `O_p(t)` can itself generate oscillatory signals even for a time-independent quantum observable `O`; to fit energy differences, use time-independent operators evolving only by the transfer matrix. At discrete unit cadence, frequencies are additionally defined only modulo the sampling frequency (aliasing); the paper's `m=10` with p=3/13,5/13,8/13 requires an explicit alias convention.

## 5. Proposition G1 (gauge-invariant observer, PROVED)
Let `X_γ(U)=1-(1/2)ReTr ∏_{e∈γ} U_e^{s_e}` for a closed oriented spatial loop `γ` and choose deterministic real anchor weights `w_p(x)` defined on the periodic quotient, independent of `U` and Euclidean time. Then `O_p(U)=Σ_x w_p(x)X_{γ_x}(U)` is gauge invariant and a bounded real multiplication operator on `L²(SU(2)^E)` and the invariant subspace. Proof: closed-loop holonomy transforms by conjugation; trace invariant; finite linear combination. For coefficients normalized to sum one and nonnegative, `0≤O_p≤2`. The weights need not coincide with literal Conway worms. The original Stage 26 FULL operators follow as a special case of geometry-only averaging provided the exact catalogue weights and families are retained. **No energy gap follows from boundedness or gauge invariance.**

## 6. Proposition T1 (positive transfer for a weighted Wilson temporal class, CONDITIONAL PROOF)
Take a finite spatial graph, compact link space `X=SU(2)^E`, Haar product measure, physical projection `P` given by site-gauge averaging. Assume a one-time-slice action with (i) *time-independent* real gauge-invariant spatial potential `V(U)` and (ii) temporal ribbons in temporal gauge yielding an independent link class kernel
`K(U,V)=∏_{e∈E} exp[ β_e (1/2)ReTr(U_e V_e^{-1}) ]`, with **β_e ≥ 0** constant across time and consistent at the periodic temporal seam. Then on `H_phys=P L²(X)` define
`T=P M_{e^{-V/2}} C_K M_{e^{-V/2}} P |_(H_phys)`, where `C_K f(U)=∫ K(U,V)f(V)dV` (normalization inessential). The kernel factor for SU(2) is a central positive-type function: parameterizing g by eigenangle θ, `exp(β cos θ)` has nonnegative Peter–Weyl character coefficients
`a_j(β)=I_{2j}(β)-I_{2j+2}(β) = [2(2j+1)/β] I_{2j+1}(β) ≥ 0` for β>0, up to chosen Haar normalization (j=0,1/2,1,...). At β=0, only the trivial coefficient survives. Consequently `C_K` is positive self-adjoint as an operator (a product of commuting positive convolution operators), `T=B* C_K B` after accounting for gauge projection and bounded real multiplication is positive and self-adjoint on `H_phys`. `K` is strictly positive pointwise for all finite β and the finite compact space; with real bounded V, the physical transfer is positivity improving on nonnegative invariant functions and compact for β_e>0, so its maximal eigenvalue is simple and isolated. This yields a finite-volume gap **if** there is a nonzero next transfer eigenvalue and the physical spectrum is defined in the usual normalized way; no uniform infinite-volume or continuum bound follows.

Caveat 1: the group-character expansion here differs from a heat-kernel temporal action; the transfer model is Wilson, not an invented replacement. Caveat 2: an unverified anisotropic action can violate the assumed single-slice factorization, especially with time-dependent or sign-changing couplings, timelike seam twists, additional mixed terms, or different plaquette normalization. Gate D is passed for the stated class of factorized Wilson actions, **not automatically for the exact production implementation** without inspecting all its actual temporal face indices and `beta` entries.

## 7. Counterexample T2 (positive Boltzmann weights do not imply positive transfer)
For U(1), `k(θ)=1-ε cos θ` is strictly positive for `0<ε<1` but its first nontrivial Fourier coefficient is `-ε/2`, so convolution by k has a negative eigenvalue. Positive pointwise kernel ≠ positive-semidefinite transfer. The Wilson SU(2) proof must use nonnegative character coefficients, not merely `exp(-S)>0`.

Counterexample T3 (reflection symmetry needed): if the temporal couplings or spatial potential differ asymmetrically across reflection-related slices, one cannot identify a single self-adjoint `T` from the above factorization without further hypotheses. Nonnegative heterogeneous couplings **on spatial edges**, held constant in Euclidean time, are compatible with T1; geometric heterogeneity alone is not a disproof of reflection positivity.

## 8. Proposition J3 (compatible helix observables, but no spectral intertwining)
If the physical transfer `T` from T1 exists and `O_p` from G1 is a bounded, time-independent, gauge-invariant real multiplier, it defines `C_pq(n)=⟨Ω,(O_p-⟨O_p⟩) (T/λ0)^n (O_q-⟨O_q⟩) Ω⟩` for the normalized positive top eigenfunction Ω. For p=q, the spectral measure is nonnegative and supported on energy gaps of *the same T*. Changing p changes spectral **weights**, not energy levels. The map `J` on geometric R⁴ does not by itself induce an operator `J_phys` on `H_phys`, nor does it intertwine with `T`. A genuine helical mass mechanism would require an explicitly defined map `I` from the geometric/C5 representation to gauge-invariant observables or states, prove gauge-covariance, boundedness/domain, compatibility with the physical time reflection, and an actual comparison/commutation relation with the transfer generator. Without that, 3:5:8 remains a testable *observer signal*, not a mass theorem.

## 9. Counterexample to a Yang–Mills gap from graph-Laplacian holonomy coercivity
The Level I graph connection Laplacian `L_U` acts on fundamental spinors in one *fixed* connection background; the pure-gauge transfer Hamiltonian acts on gauge-invariant **functions of all links**. These are different spaces. At identity links, `L_I` has covariantly constant zero modes. The finite-coupling Wilson measure assigns positive probability to every open neighborhood of identity links; it cannot enforce a uniform deterministic local frustration threshold. The conditional I-A/B gap is therefore not an almost-sure gap theorem for the unrestricted Wilson ensemble. Moreover even a physical finite-volume positive transfer gap is not uniform as spatial volume or lattice cutoff is removed.

## 11. Decisions
**Proved now:** J on geometric C²; pullback helix is kinematic; weighted closed-loop functions are gauge-invariant; T1 is positive for the explicit time-independent factorized SU(2) Wilson action class (character expansion). **Refuted as deductions:** geometric J alone supplies quantum Wick rotation; geometric frequencies alone impose YM mass ratios; deterministic Level I frustration holds over the whole Wilson ensemble; positive Boltzmann weight alone implies operator positivity. **Still open:** literal Stage 26 action equivalence to T1; nontrivial J-to-physical transfer intertwiner; infinite-volume or continuum mass gap; scalar mass/string-tension continuum ratio.
